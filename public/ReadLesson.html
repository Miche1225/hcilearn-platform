HCI 2 lessons, from the basics of HCI to smart environments.

Learning Objectives

Define Human-Computer Interaction and explain its interdisciplinary nature.
Identify key concepts from HCI 1 (usability, user-centered design, heuristics, etc.).
Discuss the evolution and significance of HCI in modern systems.
Reflect on past HCI learnings and identify areas for deeper exploration in HCI 2.
Explain the principles of Design Thinking and UCD.
Describe and apply the stages of the UCD process.
Identify user needs through interviews, observations, and empathy-building.
Collaborate on low-fidelity design solutions based on user insights.
Define mental models and explain their role in HCI.
Describe key cognitive models (e.g., GOMS, KLM, Norman’s Model of Interaction).
Analyze how cognitive and mental models affect user performance and error.
Apply models to evaluate or redesign real-world user interfaces.
Identify the role and purpose of prototyping in the design process.
Use Figma or Adobe XD to create interactive UI prototypes.
Differentiate between low-, mid-, and high-fidelity prototypes.
Apply best practices in layout, navigation, and interaction design.
Collaboratively design and share a working prototype using modern tools.
Explain the role of user research in the HCI process.
Plan and conduct basic user interviews.
Synthesize qualitative data into clear, actionable personas.
Create journey maps to visualize the user experience across touchpoints.
Apply ethical research practices during data collection and reporting.
Define accessibility and inclusive design in the context of HCI.
Identify common barriers faced by users with different disabilities.
Apply WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) to evaluate websites or apps.
Redesign or recommend changes to improve accessibility.
Reflect on the ethical and societal impact of accessible technology.
Identify ethical concerns in user research, data handling, and interface design.
Describe how cultural differences affect user needs and expectations.
Apply ethical frameworks (e.g., ACM Code of Ethics, utilitarianism, virtue ethics) to HCI cases.
Analyze HCI designs for potential cultural bias or ethical violations.
Propose ethically and culturally sensitive design solutions.
Explain the role of emotion and motivation in user experience design.
Identify emotional triggers and motivators in digital interfaces.
Apply frameworks such as Norman’s Emotional Design and Self-Determination Theory to interface evaluation.
Design or critique interfaces with attention to emotional and motivational impact.
Reflect on how emotional and motivational design can shape user behavior.
Explain the role of AI in conversational and voice interfaces.
Identify components, types, and examples of conversational interfaces (chatbots, virtual assistants).
Differentiate Voice UI (VUI) from graphical UI and describe usability issues.
Create a simple prototype of a conversational or voice interface for a real-world scenario.
Assess user experience in conversational and voice interfaces.
Explain the concepts of Mixed Reality (MR), Augmented Reality (AR), Virtual Reality (VR), and Tangible Interfaces.
Identify real-world applications of AR/VR in education, business, healthcare, and entertainment.
Differentiate AR and VR and identify usability challenges in both.
Design a simple AR/VR or tangible interface concept for a real-world scenario.
Assess user experience in AR/VR and tangible interfaces.
Explain the concept of Ubiquitous Computing (UbiComp) and Smart Environments.
Identify examples of smart environments in daily life (smart homes, healthcare, cities, workplaces).
Differentiate between traditional computing vs. ubiquitous computing in terms of interaction.
Design a simple concept for a UbiComp/smart environment application.
Assess usability, privacy, and ethical issues in UbiComp systems.
